## What makes a cryptocurrency exchange worth using?

Before looking at brands, it helps to separate the features that affect day-to-day use from the features that mainly look good on a homepage.

- **Legal and regional availability**
- **Trading fees and spreads**

- **Deposit and withdrawal methods**

- **Spot, margin, futures, options, and other product access**

- **Quality of order types and trading tools**

## Spot trading, derivatives, and funding costs

Spot trading is the simplest product category. You buy or sell the underlying cryptocurrency, and there is no leverage by default. Trading fees apply when an order fills.

Margin and derivatives are more complicated. OKX documentation distinguishes spot, spot margin, perpetual futures, delivery futures, and options. Its unified-account materials also describe different account modes for combining spot and derivatives positions.

Perpetual futures do not expire, but they use funding payments to help keep the contract price aligned with the underlying market. Funding is exchanged between traders rather than functioning as a standard trading commission. The rate and settlement interval can vary by contract.

That creates several costs that a new trader may overlook:

- Trading commission when the order fills
- Bid-ask spread
- Funding payment for perpetual positions
- Borrowing interest for margin
- Withdrawal or network fees
- Liquidation-related losses if margin becomes insufficient
- Slippage during volatile markets

Leverage does not make a trade cheaper. It increases the size of the position relative to the margin and can make losses arrive faster. If the goal is simply to buy and hold cryptocurrency, spot trading is usually easier to understand than derivatives.
